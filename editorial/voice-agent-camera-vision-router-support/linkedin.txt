"The internet light? It's kind of orange. Is it flashing? I think it's flashing."

Every ISP support line hears that a few thousand times a day. Asking the caller to point their phone at the router fixes it in one second. Streaming that camera into the model all call long is how you turn it into a cost problem.

What we built instead:
→ looking is a tool the model calls, with a reason it has to state ("confirm the power cycle worked")
→ the camera is read only during a look: three frames, 0.35 s apart
→ the three frames go to the vision model as one side-by-side image
→ the vision model returns light states in a fixed vocabulary, and a Python table picks the fix

The part I would reuse everywhere: one frame cannot see a blink. A status light between flashes looks off in a photo, and the agent sends the caller to check a cable that is fine. If the state you care about changes over time, put time inside the image.

Two looks send six frames. A two-minute call produces 1,800 on camera.

The router rescue demo is live in our playground. Use your camera or our drawn router, break it, and watch every frame the agent chose to look at.
