"Thanks, that's all." Most support agents send that to their biggest model, with a 4,000-token prompt in front of it.

A voice agent's model bill is calls times turns times prompt size. One model for every turn means frontier rates for "okay" and "can you hear me".

What we built instead, on LiveKit:
→ every caller turn is embedded once, and that one vector does two jobs
→ it looks the question up in a semantic answer cache, and a hit is spoken with no model call
→ otherwise it picks the cheapest model whose example turns it resembles: nano for small talk, mini for account questions, GPT-4.1 for disputes
→ dispute words always escalate, and only GPT-4.1 can move money

The one I would copy first: tools follow the tier. In our first test run, "take off the duplicate" routed to the tiny model as small talk, and it issued the credit. Now a routing mistake can cost a better answer, never a wrong refund.

One real eight-turn call: $0.0032 in model spend, against $0.0118 on GPT-4.1 for every turn. The dispute still ran on GPT-4.1, because it should.
