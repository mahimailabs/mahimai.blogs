"Rx forty-four seventy-one D." The label says B.

Refill lines fail on single characters. B against D. Metformin against metoprolol. Most voice agents hear the code, say "got it", and move on.

What a pharmacy assistant does, in code:
→ every field is captured as "heard", and only counts once the caller confirms the read-back
→ the tool checks the value against the file and answers "match" or "no match", never the right answer
→ codes are rewritten in the TTS node, so 4471-B is spoken as "four, four, seven, one, B as in Bravo"
→ the transcript is redacted before any line is logged: dates of birth, phone numbers, postal codes

The one I would copy first: keep the record out of the model's context. If the tool returned the correct Rx number on a mismatch, the agent would helpfully read it out, and your refill line becomes a lookup service for anyone who guesses.
