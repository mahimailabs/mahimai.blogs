1/ A video avatar on a LiveKit voice agent is not a skin on the voice. It is a third participant in the room. That one fact changes how interruptions, transcripts and latency work.

2/ Your agent streams TTS audio to the avatar over a byte stream. The avatar publishes audio and video together, from one process, so WebRTC keeps the lips on the voice.

3/ Interruptions: the audio to stop is already in the avatar's buffer. The agent sends lk.clear_buffer, and the face has to stop, not just your track.

4/ Transcripts: the avatar answers with lk.playback_finished and the position it reached. Without that, your agent "remembers" words the guest never heard.

5/ Test: interrupt mid-sentence, then ask "what did you just say?" Words the face never spoke means the transcript is cut at the sender.

6/ Latency: AvatarMetrics gives join latency and playback latency. Both sit after TTS and are invisible in STT, LLM and TTS numbers.

7/ We put a hotel concierge with an Anam avatar in our playground. Cut it off and watch the numbers: https://mahimai.ca/blog/livekit-avatar-concierge-lip-sync
