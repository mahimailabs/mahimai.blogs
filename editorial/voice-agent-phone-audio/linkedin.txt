Your voice agent passed every test in Chrome. Then a storm hit and the calls came in from dark kitchens on one bar of signal.

The phone network does three things to your audio. It cuts it to 8 kHz. It squeezes every sample into 8 bits. And on a weak cell signal it drops or delays about one packet in six, in bursts.

The third one is the expensive one. A dropped packet looks like a pause, so the endpointer thinks the caller finished in the middle of their account number.

We measured it on a simulated line (Whisper base.en, 40 LibriSpeech utterances):
→ web audio: 10.0% word error rate
→ landline, 8 kHz mu-law: 11.8%
→ bad cell: 15.5%
→ bad cell with an adaptive jitter buffer, FEC and loss concealment: 11.5%

The codec costs less than people think. The network is what hurts, and most of it is recoverable on the receive side.

We built this into a playground demo: a storm outage line where you move your own call onto a bad cell line, read your account number, and hear how your voice reached the agent.
